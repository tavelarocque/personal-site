Rust · three-party MPC · research prototype

darkmpc

A dark pool its operator cannot see into. Traders split each order into three secret shares, one pair per server, and the servers run the auction together without any of them seeing an order. Type an order below and see exactly what each server gets.

Research prototype, not audited. Secure only if at most one of the three servers is dishonest.

1

Split an order

Every 64-bit value is drawn as an 8 by 8 grid of dots, one per bit. Your order is readable, in amber. Each field is split into three pieces that add back up to it modulo 264; two of them are fresh random words from your browser's cryptographic generator, and the third makes the sum come out right.

Side

Symbols are shared one bit at a time, as darkmpc's client does; this demo has four symbols, so two bits, where the benchmarks use 13. Side is 0 for buy and 1 for sell.

2

Three servers, two pieces each

Server i receives pieces xi and xi+1 of every field: replicated secret sharing. Any two servers together hold all three pieces; any one alone holds two uniformly random words.

View as

Pick a server to sit in its seat.

3

Match in the dark

Your order joins a small batch from other traders. Stage 1 is a midpoint auction: per symbol, matched volume is the smaller of total buys and total sells, each side fills in arrival order, and an order whose fill would land below its minimum is removed and the allocation repeats, at most 3 passes. The servers compute it on shares and send each trader only the pieces of their own fill.

Honest about this step: the shares are real, but your browser is not running the three-party protocol. The fills come from a JavaScript port of darkmpc's reference matcher (crates/reference), identical to the Rust code on 5,000 random batches. darkmpc's MPC auction reproduces that reference exactly in its own property tests.

4

Where the guarantee ends

One thing the cryptography cannot stop, and one thing it catches.

Two servers collude

Choose two servers to pool what they hold. Together they have all three pieces, so your order reappears. Nothing in darkmpc prevents this: it assumes at most one of the three servers is dishonest, which is why the servers are meant to be run by three independent organisations.

One server cheats

In malicious mode, whenever the servers open a value, each one receives its missing piece from both servers that hold it and aborts if the two copies differ. Make a server send a wrong copy of the piece it holds.

In short

A dark pool hides orders from the market but not from its operator. darkmpc removes the operator's view by splitting every order across three servers that compute the auction together with secure three-party computation. No single server sees an order, a total or a fill, not even which stocks are traded. Two colluding servers would see everything, and the batch size is public. The design report lists every limit.
