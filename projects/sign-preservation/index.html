Sign preservation with reuse

A forecaster that announces probabilities is calibrated if things it calls 70% likely happen about 70% of the time. The best known bounds on how calibrated any forecaster can be, from Dagan et al. (STOC 2025), both pass through a small two-player game. This page lets you play it against an exactly optimal opponent.

The rules and every value here come from the public code, spr-calibration-code. A write-up of the results is in preparation.

Play the Pointer

There is a row of empty cells. Each round you point at an empty cell. The Labeler may first erase any minus signs to its left and any plus signs to its right, then writes a plus or a minus in your cell. Erased cells can be used again. After the last round, you score one point for every sign still on the board, and you may stop early to keep what you have. The Labeler here plays perfectly.

      Why this game

      How fast the best possible calibration error can grow over T predictions is an open problem. Dagan, Daskalakis, Fishelson, Golowich, Kleinberg and Okoroafor showed that the answer is tied, in both directions, to how the value of this game grows with the board and the number of rounds, building on the sign preservation game of Qiao and Valiant. The strongest known lower bound comes from a Pointer that commits to its moves in advance and still keeps each sign with a guaranteed probability.

      The code computes the game's exact values, certifies the best such committed strategies on small boards in both directions, with exact rational strategies below and Farkas certificates above, and checks the solver against an unreduced brute force and an independent Python reference.
